# Game of Life: Multi-Player Spec

*Version 1.0 initial case: questions for reviewers.*

This is the initial case for the multiplayer layer. It is version 1.0, crude on purpose, and it will be wrong in places. Your questions are how it gets better.

## Where to start

This work has two layers, and neither is complete without the other. The single-player tradecraft, one person and one quest log, only works inside a multiplayer universe: who builds the doors, who keeps them open, and how one person's rooms can be compared with another's. Designing that universe is the open work.

- **Single-player (the on-ramp):** [github.com/mario-blandini/Game-of-Life/issues/2](https://github.com/mario-blandini/Game-of-Life/issues/2)
- **Multiplayer (the initial case):** [github.com/mario-blandini/Game-of-Life/issues/1](https://github.com/mario-blandini/Game-of-Life/issues/1)

If you're new, read the single-player version first, then the initial case. Then pick one question below and answer it in a comment.

## What this is not

- Not finished. It's the first example, not the final design.
- Not a program, a therapy, or a product.
- Not a score or a ranking of people.
- Not owned by one person. Fork it, change it, argue with it.

## Not decided yet

These are deliberately left open. Take any of them, and no coding is needed.

**The big questions**

- Is a life measured by the choices a person makes, or does that quietly blame people whose rooms left them little room to choose?
- Can something be recognized and recorded without changing what it is? What happens to a good act once it has a score?
- Can young people benefit from crucibles at all? At what age, with whose help, and does telling a young person to "choose a challenge" help them, or add weight to someone who already carries too much?
- Is modern life short on purpose, and can a designed system supply it without becoming one more institution that drifts from its reason for existing?
- What lets an institution keep its original purpose across generations? Can this one be built to resist that drift, or is drift the price of lasting?
- Who decides what counts as "good," and what should happen when cultures, faiths, or generations disagree?
- Who keeps the memory of what was done, and what is lost if a few people hold the record?
- Should something like this stay small to stay honest, or must it grow to matter?

**The practical questions**

- How rooms, missions, or contributions are scored, and by whom
- How reviewers are chosen, and how disagreements are resolved
- How credit and recognition are recorded, and who holds the records
- What happens to this project if the first volunteers step away

## How to use this list

Pick any question that interests you and answer it in a comment, or tell us why it's the wrong question. You don't need to cover them all, and you don't need to be an expert. A good question or a plain "this confused me" is a contribution.

If a question here prompts a better one, **ask it.** That's the point.

## Starting questions

### Young people
- What can a young person gain from a chosen challenge that an adult cannot, and what can they not yet carry?
- How would we tell a growing young person from a young person being quietly overloaded?
- What should adults provide first (safety, rest, someone to talk to) before a challenge is offered at all?
- Is hardship a teacher at any age, or only once a person has a safe place to return to?
- Who should speak to young people about this, and who should not?

### What the case is for
- What is the one thing this is trying to do, and could you say it in a sentence? If not, what is getting in the way?
- What would count as success after a year? What would count as an honest failure?
- Who is this meant to serve, and who does it quietly overlook?
- If this were only the smallest honest version, what would remain?

### Consent and the limits of asking (load-bearing)

**The rule, fixed for version 1.0:** nothing here uses anyone's information without their clear opt-in, and anyone can withdraw it at any time. Everything else on this page is open. How to do this well is a fair question. Whether to do it is not.

- What does it mean for consent to be real and not just a checkbox?
- When is it wrong to ask someone to share something, even if they would say yes?
- What do we owe a person after they leave, or after this project ends?
- Which things, once shared, can never be taken back, and how should that change what we ask?

### Fairness and judgment
- When two reasonable people judge the same act differently, is the disagreement a flaw to remove or a truth to keep?
- Can anyone other than the person who lived it say what a hardship weighed?
- Does measuring good deeds reward doing good, or looking good? How would we tell?
- What would an injustice look like inside a system built to be fair, and who would notice first?

### Motive and reward
- What happens to the reasons people give when giving is recognized?
- Where does recognition honor someone, and where does it begin to pressure them?
- Is there a kind of good that stops being good the moment it is counted?

### Authority and disagreement
- Who should hold authority over something that belongs to everyone, and why them?
- What keeps a group of reviewers from slowly agreeing with themselves?
- When reviewers cannot agree, what does a fair outcome look like?
- Which decisions are too hard to undo to be made quickly, and which are better learned by trying?
- How does a newcomer gain a real voice and not just a place to comment?

### Who can take part
- What does a person need (time, money, standing, connections) before they can take part, and does it quietly shape who this is for?
- Would the idea hold up for someone with no budget, no organization, and no audience?
- Whose voice is missing from this conversation, and how would we know?

### Harm and its limits
- What is the worst honest way this could be used, and the worst dishonest one?
- Where should this stop and hand a person to a professional, a family, or an institution?
- What should this never claim to be able to do?

### Truth and the evidence for it
- Which claims here are measured, which are observed, and which are hopes? Is the difference visible to a reader?
- What would change your mind about this approach?
- What has already been learned about this elsewhere, in history, research, or lived practice, that we are ignoring?

### Time, scale, and succession
- What breaks, or is lost, if ten times as many people take part?
- Who carries this forward when the first volunteers are gone, and what must they be handed?
- Can someone take this up for a different cause without asking permission, and is that a risk or the point?

## Ask more

This list is a starting point, not a boundary. Before you reply, try finishing one of these sentences:

- "I don't understand why..."
- "What would happen if..."
- "Who decided that..."
- "Have you considered..."

Post your question as a comment, even if you're not sure it's a good one. Good questions often start out sounding naive.

Thank you for taking part in version 1.0.
